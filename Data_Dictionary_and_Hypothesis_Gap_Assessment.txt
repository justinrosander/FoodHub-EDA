Data Dictionary and Hypothesis Gap Assessment

Dataset reviewed
- File: data/raw/foodhub_order.csv
- Number of records: 1,898
- Number of fields: 9
- Missing values: No blank or null values were found in the file. Note that the `rating` field uses `Not given` as a business value for unrated orders rather than a missing value.

Data Dictionary

1. order_id
- Type: Integer identifier
- Description: Unique identifier for each food order.
- Role in analysis: Primary order-level key.

2. customer_id
- Type: Integer identifier
- Description: Unique identifier for the customer who placed the order.
- Role in analysis: Useful for repeat-customer analysis and customer-level behavior.

3. restaurant_name
- Type: Categorical text
- Description: Name of the restaurant fulfilling the order.
- Role in analysis: Used to measure restaurant demand, popularity, and restaurant-level performance.

4. cuisine_type
- Type: Categorical text
- Description: Cuisine category associated with the restaurant or order, such as American, Japanese, or Italian.
- Role in analysis: Used for demand segmentation and comparison across cuisine groups.

5. cost_of_the_order
- Type: Numeric, continuous
- Description: Total dollar value of the order.
- Observed range: 4.47 to 35.41
- Role in analysis: Useful for revenue analysis, price segmentation, and order-value comparisons.

6. day_of_the_week
- Type: Categorical text
- Description: Indicates whether the order was placed on a `Weekday` or `Weekend`.
- Observed values: `Weekday`, `Weekend`
- Role in analysis: Supports demand comparisons by day type.

7. rating
- Type: Ordinal categorical
- Description: Customer-provided rating for the order. Unrated orders are labeled `Not given`.
- Observed values: `3`, `4`, `5`, `Not given`
- Role in analysis: Supports customer satisfaction analysis.
- Important note: This field does not contain ratings of `1` or `2`, which limits the ability to study the full dissatisfaction spectrum.

8. food_preparation_time
- Type: Numeric, discrete
- Description: Time in minutes taken by the restaurant to prepare the order after confirmation.
- Observed range: 20 to 35
- Average: 27.37
- Role in analysis: Measures restaurant operational efficiency and contributes to total fulfillment time.

9. delivery_time
- Type: Numeric, discrete
- Description: Time in minutes taken to deliver the order after pickup.
- Observed range: 15 to 33
- Average: 24.16
- Role in analysis: Measures delivery efficiency.

Derived variables available from the dataset
- Total fulfillment time = food_preparation_time + delivery_time
- Restaurant order volume = count of orders by restaurant_name
- Cuisine demand by day type = count of orders by cuisine_type and day_of_the_week
- Rating rate = proportion of orders where rating is not `Not given`

Hypothesis Sufficiency Assessment

Hypothesis 1
- Statement: Restaurants with higher order volumes tend to have longer average food preparation times.
- Can this dataset test it?: Yes, with moderate confidence.
- Why: The dataset includes `restaurant_name` and `food_preparation_time`, so order volume can be calculated per restaurant and compared with average preparation time.
- Key limitation: The dataset does not include kitchen capacity, staffing levels, restaurant operating hours, or exact timestamps, so any observed relationship would be correlational rather than causal.

Hypothesis 2
- Statement: Orders with longer total fulfillment times are less likely to receive high customer ratings.
- Can this dataset test it?: Partially.
- Why: A total fulfillment time metric can be derived by adding `food_preparation_time` and `delivery_time`, and this can be compared against the available `rating` values.
- Key limitations:
- A large share of orders is unrated (`Not given`, 736 orders), which may bias results.
- Ratings only appear as `3`, `4`, or `5`, with no `1` or `2` values, so the dataset does not capture the full range of low satisfaction.
- The file does not include other satisfaction drivers such as food quality, order accuracy, packaging quality, customer expectations, delivery distance, or weather conditions.
- Conclusion: The dataset is enough for a directional exploratory test, but not for a strong or complete validation of this hypothesis.

Hypothesis 3
- Statement: Demand patterns vary by day type, with weekends generating higher order volumes for certain cuisines and restaurants than weekdays.
- Can this dataset test it?: Yes.
- Why: The dataset includes `day_of_the_week`, `cuisine_type`, and `restaurant_name`, which are sufficient to compare weekday versus weekend order counts.
- Key limitation: Day type is only captured at a high level as `Weekday` or `Weekend`; exact dates and specific weekdays are not available, so more granular temporal patterns cannot be tested.

Gap Summary

The available dataset is sufficient for exploratory analysis and for testing Hypotheses 1 and 3 reasonably well. Hypothesis 2 can only be tested partially because the rating data is incomplete and compressed into a narrow range of positive outcomes.

Main data gaps
- No exact order, confirmation, pickup, or delivery timestamps
- No geographic information such as delivery distance or neighborhood
- No operational variables such as staffing, restaurant capacity, or driver availability
- No external context such as weather, holidays, or promotions
- No low-end rating values (`1` or `2`) in the current file
- Many unrated orders are encoded as `Not given`, reducing usable sample size for satisfaction analysis

Recommendation
- Proceed with EDA using this dataset for demand, timing, and high-level satisfaction patterns.
- Treat all hypothesis testing as exploratory rather than causal.
- If stronger validation is needed, collect timestamped operational data, fuller rating coverage, and contextual features such as distance, weather, and promotions.
